### Summary of Findings
- **Pages 90–95**: Text descriptions of Solomon’s circles, seals, pentacles, and preparatory plates (no full drawings extracted in this pass).
- **Page 96**: Four major plates (Anophezeton triangle/circle, multi-ring circle with hexagrams and “Maria”, vessel/alembic with Hebrew band, additional circular seal).
- **Pages 97–100**: Tabular seals of the 72 Goetic spirits (Bael → Andromalius) – geometric sigils with crosses, lines, circles, and occasional animal forms.
- **Pages 101–102**: Blank / ruled pages (null result).
- **Pages 103–110**: Theurgia Goetia hierarchical text (emperors Carnesiel, Caspiel, Amenadiel, Demoriel and subordinates) with occasional small seals.
- **Pages 111–120**: Extensive tabular seals of Theurgic spirits under the four emperors – hundreds of unique geometric sigils.
- **Pages 121–150**: Continuation of Theurgia listings and seals (partial coverage in triple deep-dive; residual extraction available on request).

### Actors & Political / Financial Context
- **Henry Dawson Lea** (transcriber, 1843): 19th-century English occult / magical manuscript copyist. Part of the broader network of private collectors and amateur antiquarians who preserved Solomonic and Goetic material outside institutional libraries. Limited public biographical detail; activity appears private and non-commercial in the surviving record.
- **Frederick Hockley** (annotator / owner, note dated 1869): Prominent 19th-century British occultist, scryer, and manuscript collector. Member of the circle that fed into later Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn networks. Financially independent enough to amass a significant private library of magical manuscripts; annotations show scholarly and practical interest rather than purely commercial speculation. His collection later entered institutional holdings (including Wellcome).
- Cohort pattern: private transcription networks in mid-Victorian England that kept ceremonial magic texts in circulation among a small educated elite, parallel to but distinct from university or medical institutional collecting.
